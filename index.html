01 / THE COVER

Cold Rome wrecked the only 4-0 team. Give Farhan his damn credit.

A 61.84-point demolition. Farhan submitted his best possible recorded lineup and took Ivan’s unbeaten record.

Full story2 min read ＋

Cold Rome made 4-0 look fucking ordinary.

The only undefeated team walked into Week 5 and lost 173.24 to 111.40. Cold Rome handed Ivan the largest defeat in First Take through five weeks. Farhan is 4-1, Ivan is 4-1, and Rome now leads him by 6.86 season points. Ivan had the perfect record. Farhan took it away with 61.84 points to spare.

Four Rome starters beat Ivan’s whole damn lineup.

Christian Watson: 32.30. Malik Nabers: 29.10. Jared Goff: 28.94. Tyler Warren: 21.40. Together, 111.74. Ivan’s entire team finished at 111.40. Farhan’s other six starters were adding to a total that those four had already matched and passed. Puka’s 37.70 and Burrow’s 24.60 kept Ivan from an even uglier number; his remaining eight supplied 49.10. Two good performances could not make this competitive.

Farhan got every available point out of this roster.

Rome’s submitted 173.24 equals the highest position-legal score available from his recorded Week 5 player pool. No bench substitution improves it. That is earned lineup credit. It does not mean every choice was obvious before kickoff; it means the lineup Farhan actually submitted left no higher-scoring legal combination unused.

Goff, Nabers, Watson and Warren carried the scoring, while Loop added 14.00 and Minnesota 11.00. Taylor supplied 11.20, Nico 10.60, Coleman 7.70 and Brown 7.00. All ten starters contributed. Farhan assembled the roster, put this lineup in, and buried the league’s only unbeaten manager. He gets the victory lap.

Ivan cannot blame the whole thing on the Watson trade.

Take Watson’s 32.30 out of Rome’s actual total and add no replacement: Farhan still has 140.94 and wins by 29.54. That is a subtraction check, not a reconstruction of what would have happened without the trade. It shows the size of the cushion. Watson had a huge game; Rome had enough elsewhere to win comfortably even without those points.

The deal deserves its own verdict, too. Farhan has used 76.70 Watson points in four starts since acquiring him. Gianni’s original return has supplied 6.80 in his lineup. Nico, another Farhan acquisition, has delivered 41.40 across the last two wins. Rome’s roster moves are producing points that Farhan is actually using. The full trade bill follows on page three.

We picked Ivan. That aged like shit.

“Editorial lean: Ivan. Moderate confidence.” That was our call. The team we picked beat only one other score in Week 5 and lost its own matchup by 61.84. We do not get to roast everyone else and quietly delete that. The original words stay on the receipt.

Ivan brought 4-0. Cold Rome handed him a 61.84-point loss.

173.24–111.40. The season’s biggest margin deserves its own page. The other four games follow in the full story.

Full story3 min read ＋

Ivan, that was an ass-kicking.

Cold Rome 173.24, ivan1265 111.40. A 61.84-point loss ends the unbeaten run. Four of Farhan’s starters supplied 111.74, enough to outscore Ivan’s entire lineup. Puka gave Ivan 37.70 and Burrow 24.60. The other eight combined for 49.10. Farhan gets to talk after this. Ivan gets to explain how the undefeated team finished second from the bottom in weekly scoring.

Raif’s season high was not remotely enough.

Glock Purdy 181.06, GWIDDYIN 133.40. Raif finally reached his own season high and still lost by 47.66. Allen and Jefferson delivered 50.30; Ameed’s Gibbs and Warren delivered 74.50. That is what hurts. Raif got a strong week from his two biggest names, and Ameed’s two backs still beat them by 24.20. Ameed even absorbed Addison’s zero. The gap is in the rest of the team, too.

Vi claimed the league. Kamar took the game.

Glen Burnie Goons 158.98, Voltron FC 144.90. Kamar started Brissett for 26.28 and got five more starters to at least 17.30. He earned the win. Vi started Deebo’s 2.50 at FLEX and benched Stevenson’s 17.50. One legal hindsight swap makes Vi a winner by 0.92. “The league mine 😭😂” is a bold claim from a manager whose own unused bench points could have changed this loss.

Gianni is rolling. Cho’s best still lost.

Pirate Flag 162.92, COKEHEADS 144.72. Washington, Pickens, Bowers and Adams gave Gianni 103.50. Four straight wins deserve respect. Cho got 32.40 from Likely and posted his season high, only to lose for the fourth straight week. I will give Gianni every bit of credit for this lineup. I will also ask why he sold Watson for a return he barely used. Both arguments fit on the same page.

Rob won. His receiver week still needs defending.

AsianPersuasion 137.76, imalik 108.20. Maye delivered 39.46, or 28.6% of Rob’s score. Wilson helped with 23.40. Lamb and Tate combined for 4.50. Rob can celebrate 3-2 without trying to sell that last number as acceptable. Ibrahim is 0-5; three legal hindsight swaps would have taken him to 138.80 and a 1.04-point win. The record is ugly, and this particular loss had a route out.

Raif has a schedule complaint. He also has a scoring problem.

740.86 points against is the harshest schedule in First Take. I am not waving that away. But Raif has beaten seven of 45 other weekly scores. Seven. He can demand sympathy for the opponents and still be asked what the hell happened to his own scoring. The schedule has made a weak start worse; it did not invent the weak start.

Ivan got the production. Raif got the names.

Their September 23 deal sent Burrow, Puka, Walker, Hampton and Loveland to Ivan; Raif received Allen, Jefferson, Kelce, Achane and Tyjae Spears. No picks or FAAB. In Weeks 3-5, Ivan used 214.42 starting points from that acquired group. Raif used 113.58 from his original group. Credit the Spears-to-Jacory-to-Wan’Dale chain and Raif reaches 120.58. He is still 93.84 behind in realized starter production. I am taking Ivan on that trade’s production so far. His loss to Rome does not give Raif those points back.

The trade that changed neither Week 5 lineup

Gianni received Jacory Croskey-Merritt; Raif received Wan'Dale Robinson on October 9 at 10:11 PM ET. No picks or FAAB. Both sat on the Week 5 bench, scoring 14.20 and 11.30. The trade got a chat announcement. It got neither manager a starting point this week.

03 / THE TRADE VERDICT

Farhan won this trade. Gianni, defend the return.

Watson: 76.70 starting points since the deal. Gianni’s original return: 6.80.

Full story3 min read ＋

Farhan won the production side. Comfortably.

On September 16 at 12:25 PM ET, Gianni sent Christian Watson to Farhan for Rome Odunze and Khalil Shakir. Watson had just put up 32.70 in Gianni’s Week 1 lineup. Farhan started him in all four games after the deal: 14.10, 22.60, 7.70 and 32.30. Total: 76.70. He bought a receiver, kept putting him in the lineup, and kept getting points. That is the part of a trade that is supposed to work.

Where the hell did Gianni’s return go?

Odunze scored 7.30 and 7.40 on Gianni’s bench. Then Gianni cut him October 3 for Higbee, cut Higbee the next day for Hockenson, and benched Hockenson’s 24.90 in Week 4. Hockenson finally started for 1.70 in Week 5. Meanwhile, Ivan claimed Odunze October 6 and used his 5.00 against Farhan. Gianni sold Watson, never started the Odunze return, and eventually watched Odunze play for someone else.

Shakir made Gianni’s Week 2 lineup for 6.80. He then became Jadarian Price in a September 23 trade, and Price became Jordyn Tyson September 26. Tyson has scored zero in all three weeks on Gianni’s roster and has not started. Gianni got two receivers back. One was cut. The other turned into a player who has supplied no points in three weeks. What exactly is the production defense for that?

I will even give Gianni the generous version.

The original return supplied 6.80 starting points. Follow the Odunze-to-Higbee-to-Hockenson waiver-slot chain and credit Hockenson’s 1.70, and the total becomes 8.50. Watson alone supplied Farhan 76.70. Through Week 5, this production comparison is a rout. These are points actually used in lineups, not a no-trade calculation of net points added or wins caused.

Four straight wins do not turn 6.80 into 76.70.

Gianni is 4-0 since the deal. I will give him all four wins. His latest lineup scored 162.92 without Watson, and that deserves respect. His remaining team has carried him; the received Watson assets have barely reached his starting lineup. Both facts count. Farhan is also 4-1 and leads Gianni by just 1.02 season points. They are close contenders with a very lopsided realized return on this particular deal.

Nico made Farhan wait. The points have arrived.

Farhan sent Montgomery to Ivan for Nico on September 16 at 2:11 AM ET. Nico had zero recorded points in Weeks 2 and 3, then supplied 30.80 and 10.60 in Farhan’s lineup: 41.40 across two wins. I like what Farhan is getting now. I am also counting the two empty weeks; the acquisition did not pay immediately.

Montgomery gave Ivan 4.40 in Week 2, then went out with Higgins and McLaurin for Achane and Jeremiyah Love. That later multi-player return cannot honestly be credited to Montgomery alone. Farhan’s acquisition is producing. The cleanest whole-deal production verdict remains Watson, where the original assets and their later use can be followed directly.

Farhan’s win was bigger than Watson.

Remove Watson’s 32.30 from the actual Week 5 lineup, without adding any replacement, and Rome still has 140.94. Ivan still loses by 29.54. That is a simple subtraction check, not an alternate history of the trade. But it tells you how thoroughly Rome beat him: Ivan cannot even dismiss this as one acquired receiver having a big day.
